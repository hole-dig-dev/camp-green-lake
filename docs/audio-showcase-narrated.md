## A/B recommendations

These are opinions from measuring the clips (loudness, frequency content, envelope, repetition), not from listening to them. You decide.

- **Wind: keep A (original).** Both clips sit in the same low range (energy mostly below about 400–500 Hz), and B is only about 3 dB louder. A has a regular, slow pulse. B's envelope is less regular, but it is a short loop, so in a long play session it may repeat noticeably. Nothing measured outweighs your preference for A. *Uncertainty:* whether B's loop is audible depends on the in-game loop length, which a 4-second clip can't show.
- **Rain: keep B (recorded, current default).** A is much brighter (spectral center about 4.9 kHz vs about 3.6 kHz) and very uneven (about 30 dB envelope swing vs about 5 dB), which usually reads as surging hiss rather than rainfall. B is steadier and darker. *Uncertainty:* low; this is the clearest of the four.
- **Dig: keep B (recorded, current default).** A's energy is almost all below about 350 Hz, mainly a thud plus tone. B has a sharp, broadband attack up to about 8 kHz, typical of a blade in dirt. *Uncertainty:* B is heard every dig, so check it doesn't get tiring when repeated quickly.
- **Metal: lean B (recorded, current default), closest call.** A is a narrow tone around 1–2 kHz. B is a brighter, sharper click (peak about 11 dB higher). B sounds more physical, but A's tone may stand out better at a distance or under wind and rain. *Uncertainty:* moderate; compare them in game with ambience playing.
